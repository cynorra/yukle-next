• For cargo arriving by vessel, the ISF Importer, meaning the goods' owner, purchaser, consignee or an agent such as a licensed customs broker, or its authorized agent must submit the Importer Security Filing in English through a CBP-approved electronic system (19 CFR 149.1, 149.2(a)).
  • Eight of the ten data elements are due no later than 24 hours before the cargo is laden aboard the vessel at the foreign port, and the container stuffing location and consolidator are due as early as possible and no later than 24 hours before arrival at a United States port (149.2(b)).
  • The filer must update the filing if information changes before the goods enter port limits, must withdraw it if the goods will no longer be imported, and must hold one of the bonds listed in 149.5(b), or have the agent post its own (149.2(d), (e); 149.5(b)).

Anyone shipping goods into the United States by sea meets a requirement that has nothing to do with duties: the Importer Security Filing, usually called ISF or "10+2". It is a set of data about the shipment that has to reach U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) before the cargo is loaded onto the ship. The usual mistake is timing, because the first deadline falls at the foreign port, often before the importer has thought about customs. This guide sets out what 19 CFR part 149 requires, who files, and the two deadlines. This is general information, not legal advice.

Who Files, and for What

The rule applies to cargo arriving by vessel. The filer is the ISF Importer, defined in 149.1(a) as the party causing goods to arrive within the limits of a U.S. port by vessel. For most shipments that is the goods' owner, purchaser, consignee or an agent such as a licensed customs broker. For immediate exportation and transportation and exportation in-bond shipments and goods bound for a foreign trade zone, it may also be the party filing that documentation, and for foreign cargo remaining on board it is the carrier or the non-vessel operating common carrier. The United States Postal Service is not an ISF Importer. The importer or its authorized agent submits the filing in English through a CBP-approved electronic interchange system (149.2(a)). The rule is written for cargo by vessel, so this guide does not cover shipments that arrive by truck or air.

The Ten Data Elements and Their Deadlines

The elements in 149.3(a) are provided for each good listed at the six-digit HTSUS number, at the lowest bill of lading level, which is the house bill of lading where there is one. The manufacturer (or supplier), country of origin and commodity number must be linked to one another at the line-item level.

Accuracy, Updates and Withdrawal

  • Information from others: where the filer receives information from another party, CBP considers how the filer acquired it under ordinary commercial practice and whether it can verify it. If it cannot reasonably verify it, the filer may submit what it reasonably believes to be true (149.2(c)).
  • Updates: the filer must update the filing if information changes, or more accurate information becomes available, after submission and before the goods enter the limits of a U.S. port (149.2(d)).
  • Flexibility on four elements: for manufacturer (or supplier), ship to party, country of origin and commodity number, an initial answer based on the best data available is permitted, then updated as soon as better data exists, and in no event less than 24 hours before arrival (149.2(f)).
  • Withdrawal: if the goods are no longer intended to be imported to the United States, the filer must withdraw the filing and send CBP the reason (149.2(e)).

Bond, Agents and Powers of Attorney

To file electronically, a party must set up the communication protocol CBP requires (149.5(a)). The ISF Importer must possess one of these bonds: a basic importation and entry bond (113.62), a basic custodial bond (113.63), an international carrier bond (113.64), a foreign trade zone operator bond (113.73) or an importer security filing bond under Appendix D to part 113. If the importer has none, the agent submitting the filing may post the agent's own bond (149.5(b)). Authorized agents must keep powers of attorney in English until revoked, and keep revoked ones and revocation letters for five years after revocation (149.5(c)). When the filing and the entry documents go in one electronic transmission, the importer provides the importer of record number, consignee number, country of origin and, if given to ten digits, the commodity number once (149.6).

Exceptions and Other Shipment Types

  • Bulk cargo that is exempt from the carrier's 24-hour cargo declaration is also exempt from the ISF (149.4(a)). Bulk cargo is homogeneous cargo stowed loose in the hold, such as oil, grain, coal, ore, or articles needing mechanical handling like lumber and steel beams (149.1(c)).
  • Break bulk cargo that is exempt from that carrier timing rule has a different timing: the ISF must be presented 24 hours before arrival in the United States, though non-qualifying break bulk and containerized cargo still follow the 24-hours-before-loading rule (149.4(b)).
  • Foreign cargo remaining on board, immediate exportation and transportation and exportation shipments use a different element list under 149.3(b): booking party, foreign port of unlading, place of delivery, ship to party and commodity number. For foreign cargo remaining on board the data is due before lading, and for in-bond shipments 24 hours before lading (149.2(b)(4), (5)).

Checklist

  • ☐ Get the vessel's loading date at booking and count back 24 hours
  • ☐ Confirm who files: the importer or its customs broker, with a power of attorney in place
  • ☐ Confirm the bond type under 149.5(b)
  • ☐ Collect the eight early elements; the manufacturer's address and the six-digit tariff number are often the missing ones
  • ☐ Calendar the stuffing location and consolidator deadline from the expected arrival date
  • ☐ Update the filing if the supplier, origin or classification changes; withdraw it if the cargo will not come

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the ISF apply to goods arriving by truck or air?

Part 149 is written for cargo arriving by vessel (149.2(a)); other modes are not covered here.

Can the ISF be filed with unknown details?

For four elements, an initial answer on the best available data is allowed and must be updated (149.2(f)). The others are due in full by the deadline.

Who is the ISF Importer if a customs broker files?

The ISF Importer is still the party defined in 149.1(a); the broker files as its authorized agent (149.2(a), 149.5).

Scope: the filing requirement in 19 CFR part 149 only. Penalties, customs entry and duties, commercial terms and the carrier-side filings are not covered. For a real shipment, a licensed customs broker can confirm how this applies. For the paperwork between a shipper and a carrier on the U.S. side, see Shipper, Carrier and Consignee: Who Does What.
