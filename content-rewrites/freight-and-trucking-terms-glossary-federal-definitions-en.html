• "Shipper" and "receiver or consignee" are defined in the safety regulations (49 CFR 390.5), "carrier" in 49 U.S.C. 13102 includes freight forwarders, and "hazardous materials" means something narrower for a CDL (placard-requiring material, 49 CFR 383.5) than in the general safety definitions.
  • Many everyday terms, including detention, deadhead, TONU, PRO number and rate confirmation, have no federal definition in the sources read for this guide, so their meaning comes from the contract or the carrier's rules.
  • The same word can be defined differently in different parts of the regulations, so a term should be read in the part that applies to the question, as the conflicts table below shows.

Freight is full of terms that people use loosely. Some are defined in federal statute or regulation, some belong to industry practice, and a few are defined differently depending on the part of the regulations. Each entry below names its source and carries one of three labels: Federal (paraphrased from the statute or regulation text), Industry (no federal definition found in the sources read), or Secondary (from a non-government source that could not be checked against a primary one). Definitions are paraphrased; follow the citation for the exact wording.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the shipper the same as the consignor?

Usually the same party. The bill of lading rule says consignor; 390.5 says shipper. Both describe the party tendering the goods.

Where do I find the legal meaning of "freight broker"?

49 U.S.C. 13102(2) and 49 CFR 371.2. See What Is a Freight Broker?

Are detention and TONU regulated?

Not in the sources read. They are contract terms.
