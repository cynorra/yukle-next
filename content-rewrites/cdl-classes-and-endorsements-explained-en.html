• CDL groups depend on the vehicle, not the job: Group A is a combination rated 26,001 lb or more with a towed unit over 10,000 lb, Group B is a single vehicle rated 26,001 lb or more, and Group C is a smaller vehicle that carries 16 or more passengers or hauls hazardous materials that require placards.
  • The skills-test vehicle can limit the license: an automatic transmission brings an E restriction, air-over-hydraulic brakes bring a Z restriction, and a pintle-hook combination brings an O restriction, so test in the vehicle you intend to drive.
  • First-time Class A, Class B, passenger, school bus and hazardous materials credentials generally require entry-level driver training from a provider on FMCSA's registry, and the hazmat endorsement also requires a TSA security determination or a valid TWIC.

Whether a driver needs a commercial driver's license, and which group, depends on the vehicle, not the job title. The federal definitions in 49 CFR part 383 turn on weight ratings, towed weight, passenger capacity and hazardous materials. The sections below sort vehicles into groups, list the endorsements and the restriction codes, and separate the three age and training rules that are often run together.

Which Group Does a Vehicle Fall Into?

GroupDefinition (383.5, 383.91)
A, combination vehicleGross combination weight rating (or weight) of 26,001 lb or more, whichever is greater, where the towed unit is rated (or weighs) more than 10,000 lb
B, heavy straight vehicleA single vehicle rated or weighing 26,001 lb or more, or such a vehicle towing a unit of 10,000 lb or less
C, small vehicleMeets neither A nor B, and is designed to transport 16 or more passengers including the driver, or is of any size and is used to transport hazardous materials

The word "hazardous materials" in Group C has a specific meaning in 383.5: material required to be placarded under subpart F of 49 CFR part 172, or any quantity of a listed select agent or toxin. A small van carrying a few labelled packages that need no placard does not fall in Group C. For when placards are required, see Hazmat Placarding. The CDL rules apply in interstate and intrastate commerce (49 CFR 390.3(b)).

Find the Group

Rules used: the greater of rating and actual weight; a combination's rating is the sum of the unit ratings; A is tested before B, and B before C. Common mistakes: treating a 26,000 lb truck and a 10,000 lb trailer as Group A (the towed unit must be over 10,000 lb), and forgetting that a heavy truck towing a small trailer is still Group B.

On a commercial learner's permit (CLP) only passenger, school bus and tank vehicle endorsements are allowed. A CLP holder with an N endorsement may operate only an empty tank vehicle, and not one that previously held hazardous materials and has not been purged (383.25). A CLP holder may not drive a commercial motor vehicle carrying hazardous materials. The hazmat endorsement has an extra federal gate: a State may not issue, renew or transfer it unless the Transportation Security Administration has determined that the individual poses no security risk warranting denial, or the individual holds a valid TWIC (383.141).

Training, Permit Age and Driving Age

  • Entry-level driver training (49 CFR 380.603, 380.605). It applies to an entry-level driver: someone who must take the CDL skills test to get a CDL for the first time, to upgrade to Class A or B, or to get a passenger or school bus endorsement for the first time. The hazmat endorsement is included for the knowledge test. The training must come from an entity on FMCSA's Training Provider Registry. Credentials issued before 7 February 2022 are not covered for that credential, and a CLP obtained on or after that date brings the training requirement. Except for the H endorsement, the theory and behind-the-wheel parts must be completed within one year of completing the first part.
  • CLP age (383.71). A person must be 18 or older, and provide proof of age, before obtaining a CLP.
  • Driving age for qualified drivers (391.11(b)(1)). To be qualified to drive a commercial motor vehicle under part 391, a person must be at least 21, except as subpart G allows. Part 391 qualifications apply to interstate commercial motor vehicle operations (390.3(a)). The part 383 text read for this guide gives no separate minimum age for driving intrastate, so a driver under 21 should check the State's rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a CDL for a vehicle under 26,001 lb?

Possibly: if it is designed for 16 or more passengers including the driver, or carries hazardous materials that require placards.

Why does my license say E?

Because the skills test was taken in an automatic. The E restriction bars manual-transmission commercial vehicles until the restriction is removed.

Is 21 the minimum age for a CDL?

Not as a single federal number. A CLP requires age 18 (383.71), and the 21-year minimum in 391.11 is a qualification for driving a commercial motor vehicle under part 391, tied to interstate operations. State rules apply to the rest.

What is the X on a license?

The code for holding both the tank vehicle and hazardous materials endorsements (383.153).
