• Out-of-service orders come from several separate rules: vehicle condition and loading (49 CFR 396.9), a driver's hours or missing records (395.13), alcohol (392.5), missing operating authority (392.9a) and an unsatisfactory safety rating (385.13), each with its own consequence and deadline.
  • An alcohol violation puts a driver out of service for 24 hours from the issuing of the order, an hours-of-service order lasts until the driver may lawfully drive again, and a vehicle order lasts until the listed repairs are done.
  • The inspection criteria used to decide an order are the North American Standard Out-of-Service Criteria, which 49 CFR 390.5 names but which are a CVSA publication this guide does not reproduce or summarize.

An out-of-service order stops a driver, a vehicle or a whole carrier operation until a stated condition is met. The orders come from different federal rules, and what ends each one differs. The Commercial Vehicle Safety Alliance (CVSA) publishes the North American Standard Out-of-Service Criteria that inspectors apply; 49 CFR 390.5 includes them in its definition of an out-of-service order. Those criteria are a copyrighted CVSA publication and could not be verified for this guide, so the numeric thresholds, such as brake-adjustment limits, are not stated here. What follows is what the federal regulations themselves say.

Imminent hazard orders are a further category: where a violation poses an imminent hazard to safety, FMCSA orders a vehicle or employee out of service or orders an employer to cease all or part of its operations (49 CFR 386.72(b)).

Driver Orders: Responsibilities

  • The carrier may not require or permit a driver under an order to operate until the driver lawfully may, and must complete the "Motor Carrier Certification of Action Taken" on the Driver/Vehicle Examination Report and deliver it to the FMCSA Division Administrator or State Director within 15 days of the examination (395.13(c)).
  • The driver must not operate while under an order, and must deliver or mail the copy of the order to the person or place the carrier designates within 24 hours (395.13(d)).
  • Drugs and other substances are barred while on duty under 392.4: Schedule I substances, amphetamines, narcotics and any substance that leaves a driver incapable of safe operation, with an exception for a substance given under a licensed practitioner's instructions where the driver was advised it will not affect safe operation.

Vehicle Orders: What to Do

  1. Leave the sticker in place. No one may remove the "Out-of-Service Vehicle" sticker before the required repairs are complete (396.9(c)(3)).
  2. Do not move it by towing. Towing counts as operating the vehicle, and only towing away by crane or hoist is excepted.
  3. Give the carrier the report at the next terminal, or send it at once if none will be reached within 24 hours (396.9(d)(1)).
  4. The carrier certifies the corrections within 15 days of the inspection and keeps a copy for 12 months (396.9(d)(3)). If the issuing State agency requests the form, it is returned to the agency.

Invented example: a driver is placed out of service for alcohol at 14:00 on 3 November 2026. The 24-hour period runs from the issuing of the order, so the driver may not drive before 14:00 on 4 November. The employer must be told by 14:00 on 4 November, a State official within 30 days (by 3 December) unless a review is sought, and a petition for review is due within 10 days (by 13 November). For vehicle paperwork details, see Roadside Inspections.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the brake and tire thresholds for an out-of-service order?

They are in CVSA's criteria, which this guide does not reproduce. Check the current CVSA publication or the inspector's report.

Can an out-of-service vehicle be driven to a repair shop?

Not under its own power. Operation includes towing, except by means of a crane or hoist (396.9(c)(2)).

What if a driver's logs for the last week are missing?

A driver required to keep a record of duty status without a current record for the day of examination and the prior seven consecutive days is subject to an order; one who lacks only the current and prior day but has the earlier days complete gets an opportunity to bring them current (395.13(b)).

Can a driver contest an order?

For an alcohol order, a written petition for review within 10 days (392.5(e)). For an operating-authority order, opportunity for review not later than 10 days after issuance (392.9a(c)).
