• A person may act as a broker or for-hire motor carrier only if registered, and a broker's registration lasts only as long as its $75,000 surety bond or trust fund, so the first check on any load is who the poster is and whether FMCSA shows them registered.
  • A broker may not work under a name other than the one on its registration and may not present itself as a carrier (49 CFR 371.7), so the company name on the rate confirmation should match the name FMCSA shows.
  • The 15-day payment rule in 49 CFR 376.12(f) governs owner-operator equipment leases, not broker-to-carrier payment, so payment timing and any quick-pay fee have to be settled in writing before booking.

A load board is an online listing service where freight is posted and carriers look for loads. This guide does not rank or recommend any board, because features and prices change and no primary source compares them. It covers what does not change as fast: how the federal rules apply to the people posting on a board, what to verify before booking, and how to read a posted rate in dollars per mile.

Who Is Posting?

PosterWhat the rules say
BrokerA person that sells, offers, negotiates or holds itself out as arranging transportation by motor carrier for compensation, and is not itself a carrier (49 U.S.C. 13102(2)). Must be registered (13901, 13904) and hold a $75,000 bond or trust fund (49 CFR 387.307).
Motor carrierA person providing motor vehicle transportation for compensation (13102(14)), registered to operate (13901).
ShipperTendering its own goods does not by itself make a shipper a broker or a for-hire carrier. It can become one if it arranges transportation for others.

The statute defines a broker by what it does, including holding itself out by advertisement as arranging transportation, so whether a given board operator or poster is a broker depends on its actual activities, which this guide does not decide.

Checks Before You Book

Broker bondA broker's registration stays in effect only as long as its $75,000 bond or trust fund does (387.307). See Freight Broker Bond Rules.
Name on the rate confirmationA broker may not perform brokerage in a name other than the one on its registration, and may not represent itself as a carrier; advertising must show broker status (371.7).
Transaction recordA broker keeps a record of each transaction for three years, showing the consignor, the carrier and its registration number, the bill of lading or freight bill number, the broker's compensation and payer, and any freight charges collected with the payment date. Claims termsNo carrier may allow less than 9 months to file or 2 years to sue (49 U.S.C. 14706(e)). See Freight Claims.

No federal rule read for this guide prescribes a rate confirmation form. Whatever the form, confirm in writing before pickup: the company's legal name, the pickup and delivery addresses and times, the rate and what it includes, any detention or quick-pay terms, the payment period, and who the paying party is.

Reading the Rate

A posted rate looks different depending on which miles it is divided by. These figures are invented to show the arithmetic. A load pays $1,800 and runs 600 loaded miles. The truck starts 120 miles from the pickup.

  • Per loaded mile: $1,800 ÷ 600 = $3.00.
  • Per total mile, empty miles included: $1,800 ÷ 720 = $2.50. This is what the truck earns for every mile it runs.
  • After an invented running cost of $1.60 per mile: 720 × $1.60 = $1,152, leaving $648.
  • After an invented 3 percent quick-pay fee ($54): $594 left, or about $0.83 per total mile.

Common mistakes: comparing two loads on the loaded-mile rate alone, forgetting the empty miles to the pickup, and leaving out the fee for faster payment. The costs that come out of the rate (fuel, tolls, insurance, maintenance, equipment payments) are yours to enter; no benchmark rate for any lane is given here because none is verified.

Payment Terms

The 15-day rule in 49 CFR 376.12(f) applies to written leases between an authorized carrier and an owner-operator who leases equipment and driving services to it: the lease must provide for payment within 15 days after the necessary delivery documents are submitted. It is not a general deadline for how fast a broker pays a carrier. Payment timing between a broker and a carrier is set by their contract. See Truck Driver Pay for the lease rules.

Box Trucks and Cargo Vans

Whether the federal safety rules apply to a small vehicle depends on its weight rating, towing and the kind of trip; see Box Trucks, Cargo Vans and Pickups. The registration question for for-hire work is separate and is set by 49 U.S.C. 13901, which has no weight line. FMCSA's registration pages explain who needs a docket number in practice and could not be re-read for this guide, so confirm your own case with FMCSA.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every poster on a board a broker?

No. Shippers, carriers and brokers all post. The category depends on what the poster does under 13102.

Does a broker have to pay me in 15 days?

Not under 376.12(f), which governs equipment leases with owner-operators. Broker payment terms come from the contract.

Can I see a broker's records of my load?

Yes. Under 371.3(c), each party to a brokered transaction has the right to review the record of that transaction.

What about fraud and double brokering?

Verified signals are the ones above: registration status, a name that matches the registration and the insurance filing, and a bond on file for a broker. Patterns beyond those were not verified from a primary source and are not listed.
