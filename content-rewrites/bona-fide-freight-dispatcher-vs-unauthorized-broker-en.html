• A broker is a person, other than a motor carrier or an employee or agent of a motor carrier, that sells, offers for sale, negotiates for, or holds itself out as selling, providing or arranging transportation by motor carrier for compensation (49 U.S.C. 13102(2)), and a broker may provide interstate brokerage only if registered under section 13904 with the financial security required by section 13906 (49 U.S.C. 14916(a)).
  • FMCSA's definition of a bona fide agent in 49 CFR 371.2 has three parts: the person is part of the carrier's normal organization, performs duties under the carrier's directions under a preexisting agreement providing a continuing relationship, and has no discretion to allocate traffic between the carrier and others.
  • Anyone who knowingly authorizes, consents to or permits unauthorized brokering is liable to the United States for a civil penalty of up to $10,000 per violation and to injured parties for all valid claims, and that liability falls jointly and severally on the entity and its officers, directors and principals (49 U.S.C. 14916(c), (d)).

Many truck dispatchers find loads and handle paperwork for owner-operators and small fleets. Federal law draws a line between that work and brokering freight, and the line decides whether a person needs broker registration and a $75,000 bond or trust fund. This guide sets out the statutory definition, the regulation's definition of a bona fide agent, and the penalty provision, with a self-check at the end. It does not decide where any individual business falls. This is general information, not legal advice.

The Broker Definition

Under 49 U.S.C. 13102(2), a broker is a person, other than a motor carrier or an employee or agent of a motor carrier, that as principal or agent sells, offers for sale, negotiates for, or holds itself out by solicitation, advertisement or otherwise as selling, providing or arranging for, transportation by motor carrier for compensation. The regulation, 49 CFR 371.2, says a broker is a person who, for compensation, arranges, or offers to arrange, the transportation of property by an authorized motor carrier.

The regulation then carves out carriers and their agents. Motor carriers, or persons who are employees or bona fide agents of carriers, are not brokers when they arrange or offer to arrange the transportation of shipments which they are authorized to transport and which they have accepted and legally bound themselves to transport (371.2).

What Makes an Agent Bona Fide

Section 371.2 defines bona fide agents as persons who are part of the normal organization of a motor carrier and perform duties under the carrier's directions pursuant to a preexisting agreement which provides for a continuing relationship, precluding the exercise of discretion on the part of the agent in allocating traffic between the carrier and others. The text of the rule breaks into parts:

Element in 371.2A question to ask
Part of the normal organization of a motor carrierDoes the person work as part of one carrier's operation, rather than as an outside intermediary?
Duties performed under the carrier's directionsWho decides which loads are taken and on what terms?
Preexisting agreement providing a continuing relationshipIs there a standing agreement made before the work, rather than one made load by load?
No discretion in allocating traffic between the carrier and othersCan the person choose which carrier gets a load, or move freight to others?

The exception in the rule is limited to shipments the carrier is authorized to transport and has accepted and legally bound itself to transport. It does not mention freight the carrier has not accepted.

The same section defines the two kinds of service a broker does: brokerage, which is the arranging of transportation or the physical movement of a motor vehicle or of property, performed for a motor carrier, consignor or consignee, and non-brokerage service, which is all other service the broker performs for them.

Registration and the Penalty Provision

A person may provide service as a broker for transportation subject to the Department's jurisdiction only if registered (49 U.S.C. 13901). The Secretary registers a person as a broker if it has sufficient experience and is fit, willing and able to comply, subject to the financial security rule (13904(a)), and each broker must employ an officer with at least 3 years of relevant experience, or provide satisfactory evidence of the officer's knowledge of related rules and practices (13904(c)). The financial security is $75,000 (13906(b)(3); 49 CFR 387.307), covered in Freight Broker Bond and Trust Fund Rules.

Section 14916(a) states the prohibition: a person may provide interstate brokerage services as a broker only if registered under 13904, in compliance with it, and with the financial security of 13906 satisfied. Three groups are excepted (14916(b)):

  • non-vessel-operating common carriers and ocean freight forwarders, when arranging inland transportation as part of an international through movement involving ocean transportation;
  • licensed customs brokers, only to the extent they are engaged in a movement under a customs bond or in customs business; and
  • indirect air carriers holding a TSA-approved Standard Security Program, only to the extent of their activities as an air carrier.

A person who knowingly authorizes, consents to or permits a violation, directly or indirectly, alone or with another, is liable to the Government for a civil penalty not to exceed $10,000 for each violation, and to the injured party for all valid claims without regard to amount (14916(c)). The liability applies jointly and severally to any corporate entity or partnership involved and to the individual officers, directors and principals of those entities (14916(d)).

Related Broker Rules

A registered broker must also keep a record of each transaction for three years (371.3), may not operate under a name other than its registration or present itself as a carrier (371.7), and may not rebate (371.9). See What Is a Freight Broker? and the dispatcher guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the regulation prohibit a dispatcher from charging a percentage?

The sections read for this guide do not address fee structures. The test in 371.2 turns on the agent's relationship with the carrier and its discretion over allocating traffic.

Can a dispatcher sign carrier-broker agreements for a carrier?

The sections read do not address signing authority. A person who signs for a carrier should have that authority in writing from the carrier.

Who is liable if unauthorized brokering occurs?

Under 14916(d), the corporate entity or partnership and the individual officers, directors and principals, jointly and severally, for a person who knowingly authorizes, consents to or permits the violation.

Scope: the three provisions above. Not covered: how FMCSA enforces the rule, case law, State law, and the application of these tests to a particular dispatch business.
