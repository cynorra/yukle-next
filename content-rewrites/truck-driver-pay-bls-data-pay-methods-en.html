• BLS puts the median pay of heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers at $58,640 a year, or $28.19 an hour, for May 2025, with the lowest tenth under $40,140 and the highest tenth over $79,380, but its survey leaves out self-employed workers.
  • BLS says these drivers are usually paid by the mile plus bonuses, so time that produces no miles, such as waiting at a dock, earns nothing under per-mile pay unless the pay agreement covers it.
  • For a leased owner-operator, 49 CFR 376.12 requires the lease to state the pay, to promise payment within 15 days of the delivery documents, and to list every charge-back with how each is computed.

"How much do truck drivers make" has no single answer, because the number depends on who is counted, how the driver is paid and what costs come out of it. The sections below give the federal wage figures with their reference dates, the pay methods the federal sources actually name, and the lease terms that decide what an owner-operator really receives.

What the Federal Data Shows

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Occupational Outlook Handbook covers heavy and tractor-trailer truck drivers. Wage figures come from BLS's Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) program for May 2025. Job counts are for 2025 and projections run 2025 to 2035. BLS also lists May 2025 median annual wages by industry: $60,320 in truck transportation, $58,550 in wholesale trade, $56,570 in manufacturing and $56,080 in construction. The $28.19 hourly figure is what $58,640 ÷ 2,080 hours works out to, which is arithmetic, not evidence that most drivers are paid by the hour.

What the figures leave out. BLS states that the pay data do not include self-employed workers, because OEWS does not collect them, and its employment-by-industry list shows 7 percent of these drivers as self-employed. The wage figures therefore describe employees and do not describe an owner-operator's income after fuel, insurance and equipment costs. A median is also the middle of the distribution: half earned less and half more, and the gap between the 10th and 90th percentile here is about $39,000 a year.

How Drivers Are Paid

BLS says drivers of heavy trucks and tractor-trailers usually are paid by the miles they have driven, plus bonuses; the per-mile rate varies by employer and may depend on cargo type and driver experience; and some long-distance drivers, especially owner-operators, are paid a share of the revenue from shipping. For leased owner-operators, 49 CFR 376.12(d) adds the forms a lease may use: a percentage of gross revenue, a flat rate per mile, a rate that varies by direction or commodity, or another agreed method.

The Arithmetic, With Invented Numbers

These figures are made up to show the calculation and are not market rates.

  • Mileage pay. A driver paid $0.60 per mile for 2,500 paid miles earns $1,500 before deductions. At 2,000 paid miles the same rate gives $1,200.
  • Revenue share and a settlement. An owner-operator on an 80 percent lease hauls a $3,000 load and is credited 0.80 × $3,000 = $2,400. Suppose the lease allows a $600 fuel advance and a $100 escrow deposit as charge-backs: $2,400 − $600 − $100 = $1,700 on the settlement, before the owner-operator pays insurance, equipment and maintenance. That is why a revenue share cannot be compared directly with an employee's wage.

Red flags to ask about: a charge-back not listed in the lease, a payment period longer than 15 days, a document the carrier requires before paying that is not logbooks or shipper-payment paperwork, and an escrow with no stated accounting.

Overtime

Whether a driver is owed overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act turns on facts. 29 CFR 782.2 explains that the motor carrier exemption depends on two things: the employer must be a carrier whose transportation is subject to the Secretary of Transportation's jurisdiction, and the employee's work must be of a kind directly affecting the safety of operation of motor vehicles in interstate commerce, which the rule defines as the work of drivers, driver's helpers, loaders and mechanics. A driver who is unsure should read the regulation and ask the U.S. Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division or an employment lawyer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is $58,640 the average truck driver salary?

No, it is the median, and it covers wage-and-salary employees only. Half of employees earned less. Self-employed drivers are not in the figure.

Are truck drivers paid by the hour or by the mile?

BLS says heavy truck and tractor-trailer drivers usually are paid by the mile plus bonuses. Other arrangements exist. The $28.19 hourly figure BLS prints equals the annual median divided by 2,080 hours; it does not show how most drivers are paid.

How fast must an owner-operator be paid?

The lease must provide for payment within 15 days after the necessary delivery documents are submitted, under 376.12(f).

Can the carrier deduct anything it likes?

Only items the lease lists, with a stated method of computing each (376.12(h)). Cargo or property damage deductions need a written explanation and itemization first.

Scope: BLS does not break these figures out by carrier, region or lane. Light-truck and delivery drivers are a separate BLS occupation. Tax treatment, per diem and a full owner-operator cost model are outside this guide, and none of it is legal advice. Related: CDL classes and endorsements.
