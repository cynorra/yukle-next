• A carrier's USDOT status, operating authority, insurance filing and safety rating can all be checked for free on two FMCSA sites, SAFER and Licensing and Insurance, using only its USDOT or MC number.
  • The insurance endorsement and surety bond must be issued in the carrier's exact registered name (49 CFR 387.15), so a name on the rate confirmation that differs from the name FMCSA shows is a reason to ask questions before loading.
  • An "unrated" safety rating only means FMCSA has not rated the carrier, while an "unsatisfactory" rating generally bars it from operating, so the two should never be read the same way.

Federal rules make several facts about a motor carrier public: whether it is registered, whether it holds operating authority, what insurance is on file and how FMCSA has rated it. This guide shows where each fact lives, what each screen field means, which findings are warning signs, and when to stop. It does not rank or recommend any company.

Safety Ratings in Plain Terms

Under 385.3, a satisfactory rating means the carrier has adequate safety management controls for its size and type of operation. Conditional means its controls are not adequate to ensure compliance, in a way that could lead to the occurrences listed in 385.5. Unsatisfactory means they are not adequate and that has resulted in those occurrences. Unrated means only that FMCSA has not assigned a rating. For carriers rated through reviews, the prohibition on operating starts on the 61st day after a notice of a proposed unsatisfactory rating, or the 46th day for carriers of placarded hazardous materials or passengers, and FMCSA may allow up to 60 additional days for a carrier making a good-faith effort to improve (385.13). A final unsatisfactory rating leads to an out-of-service order and revocation of operating authority.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a company with no trucks have to be a broker?

If it sells or arranges transportation by a motor carrier without being the motor carrier, 13102(2) calls it a broker. SAFER's power-unit count is a hint, not proof, so ask in writing who will haul.

Is "unrated" a bad sign?

Not by definition. Under 385.3 it means FMCSA has not assigned a rating, which tells you no rating exists to rely on.

Can a carrier refuse to show proof of insurance?

387.7(e) makes proof of financial responsibility public information, to be produced on reasonable request.

Does the $750,000 minimum cover cargo?

The 387.9 table sets public-liability minimums. Cargo insurance is a separate matter; see the insurance guide.
