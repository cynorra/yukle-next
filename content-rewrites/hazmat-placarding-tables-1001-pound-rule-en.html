• A transport vehicle carrying any quantity of a hazardous material must be placarded on each side and each end with the placards that Tables 1 and 2 of 49 CFR 172.504(e) name for it, unless an exception in that section or in 172.500 applies (172.504(a)).
  • Table 1 materials need their placard in any quantity, while Table 2 materials on a highway vehicle need no placard when the vehicle holds less than 454 kg (1,001 pounds) aggregate gross weight of them, an exception that does not cover bulk packagings or materials subject to 172.505 (172.504(c)).
  • The shipper must give the motor carrier the required placards, and a motor carrier may not transport the hazardous material in a vehicle that lacks them (172.506(a)).

Placarding is the rule that puts the diamond signs on the sides of a truck. It is one section of the federal hazardous materials regulations, 49 CFR 172.504, and most mistakes come from two points: which table a material sits in, and how the 1,001-pound threshold is counted. This guide follows the order in which the rule applies, then works four invented loads through it. It is separate from the hazardous materials endorsement a driver needs on a CDL. This is general information, not legal advice.

The Basic Rule

Under 172.504(a), each bulk packaging, freight container, unit load device, transport vehicle or rail car containing any quantity of a hazardous material must be placarded on each side and each end, with the placards that Tables 1 and 2 of the section specify. The rule applies to each person who offers a hazardous material for transportation and to each person who transports it (172.500(a)). A placard has to represent a hazard of the material actually being carried, and no sign or slogan that could be confused with a placard may be displayed (172.502(a)). Placards may be shown even when not required, if they otherwise meet the subpart (172.502(c)).

The 1,001-Pound Exception

Under 172.504(c), for highway and rail, placards are not required on a transport vehicle or freight container that holds less than 454 kg (1,001 pounds) aggregate gross weight of Table 2 materials. Three limits matter:

  • It covers Table 2 materials only. Any quantity of a Table 1 material brings the placard back.
  • It does not apply to bulk packagings or to materials subject to 172.505.
  • The weight is the aggregate gross weight of all Table 2 materials on the vehicle, so different categories add together.

The section adds that the exception does not stop an operator from displaying placards on a vehicle that does not need them, unless 172.502 prohibits it.

Step by Step

  1. Is the material outside the placarding subpart altogether? Infectious substances, limited quantities identified as such, materials prepared under 173.13, small quantities under 173.4, 173.4a and 173.4b, and combustible liquids in non-bulk packagings are outside it (172.500(b)).
  2. Is any material in Table 1? If so, placard it, in any quantity.
  3. Is any package a bulk packaging, or a material under 172.505 (inhalation-hazard poisons and dangerous-when-wet subsidiary hazards, for example)? If so, the weight exception does not apply to it.
  4. Add the aggregate gross weight of the remaining Table 2 materials. Below 454 kg (1,001 pounds), no placard is required for them on a highway vehicle. At that figure or above, each category needs its Table 2 placard.
  5. Check whether the DANGEROUS placard or another exception in 172.504(b) and (f) can be used.

Other Provisions in the Section

  • DANGEROUS placard: a freight container, unit load device, transport vehicle or rail car with non-bulk packages of two or more Table 2 categories needing different placards may display DANGEROUS instead (172.504(b)), subject to the 1,000 kg limit above.
  • Several Class 1 divisions: only the placard for the lowest division number must be displayed (172.504(f)(1)).
  • FLAMMABLE for COMBUSTIBLE: allowed on a cargo tank or portable tank (172.504(f)(2)).
  • Poison placards: in domestic transportation, a POISON INHALATION HAZARD placard is not required on a vehicle already showing POISON GAS (172.504(f)(8)), and a POISON placard is not required on one showing either of those two (172.504(f)(11)).
  • Residue: a non-bulk packaging holding only the residue of a Table 2 material need not be counted, except for materials subject to 172.505 (172.504(d)(1)).
  • Subsidiary hazards: a vehicle with a poisonous inhalation-hazard material needs POISON INHALATION HAZARD or POISON GAS placards in addition to any others, and a vehicle with a dangerous-when-wet subsidiary hazard needs DANGEROUS WHEN WET placards (172.505(a), (c)).

Who Provides the Placards, and How They Are Displayed

Each person offering a hazardous material to a motor carrier for highway transport must provide the required placards before or when the material is offered, unless the carrier's vehicle is already placarded for it. No motor carrier may transport it unless the placards are affixed (172.506(a)). Each placard must be clearly visible from the direction it faces, may be placed on the front of a truck-tractor instead of the cargo body, must be clear of ladders, doors and tarpaulins, and must be kept legible and free of damage and dirt (172.516). A diamond placard measures at least 250 mm (9.84 inches) on each side (172.519(c)(1)). A bulk packaging that needed placards must stay placarded when emptied unless it has been cleaned and purged or refilled as the section allows (172.514(b)).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 1,001-pound figure per product or per vehicle?

It is the aggregate gross weight of all Table 2 materials on the vehicle or freight container, so categories add together (172.504(c)).

Does a driver need a hazardous materials endorsement?

Part 383 defines hazardous materials as those designated under 49 U.S.C. 5103 and required to be placarded under subpart F of part 172 (383.5), so the endorsement question follows the placarding question. The endorsement rules themselves are in CDL Classes and Endorsements.

Are Class 9 materials placarded?

Not for domestic transportation, but bulk packagings still carry the identification number (172.504(f)(9)).

Scope: highway transport under 172.504 and the sections named above. Packaging, shipping papers, marking, labelling, other modes and State rules sit elsewhere in the regulations and are not covered. Carrier-specific questions are best put to PHMSA or a hazardous materials compliance professional.
