• A carrier must request each new driver's motor vehicle record from every licensing authority that issued the driver a license or permit in the previous 3 years, and investigate the driver's safety performance history with DOT-regulated employers over the same 3 years, with both on file within 30 days of the start of employment (49 CFR 391.23(a)-(c)).
  • The Pre-Employment Screening Program (PSP) is a separate, optional service created by 49 U.S.C. 31150 that gives screening providers access to crash and serious driver-related inspection reports, and it may be used only during the pre-employment assessment and only with the applicant's written consent (31150(b), (c)).
  • When a consumer reporting agency supplies a report, the Fair Credit Reporting Act adds its own steps: a stand-alone written disclosure and written authorization before the report is obtained, and a copy of the report and a statement of rights before any adverse action (15 U.S.C. 1681b(b)(2), (3)).

Hiring a commercial driver means checking several records, from different sources, under different rules. Some are required by regulation, one is an optional federal service, and one set of privacy rules applies whenever a background-check company is involved. This guide separates them: what 49 CFR 391.23 requires, what the PSP is under 49 U.S.C. 31150, what rights the driver has, and where the Fair Credit Reporting Act fits. This is general information, not legal advice.

What 391.23 Requires of the Carrier

For each driver it employs, a carrier must make two inquiries (391.23(a)):

  1. An inquiry, within 30 days of the date employment begins, to each licensing authority where the driver held or holds a license or permit during the preceding 3 years, to obtain the driver's motor vehicle record covering the prior 3-year driving history.
  2. An investigation of the driver's safety performance history with Department of Transportation regulated employers during the preceding three years.

A copy of each motor vehicle record goes in the driver qualification file within 30 days of the start date. If a licensing authority sends no record, the carrier documents a good-faith effort (391.23(b)). Replies from previous employers, or documentation of good-faith efforts to get them, go in the driver investigation history file within the same 30 days. Any time the driver needs to exercise their due process rights is separate from that 30 days (391.23(c)(1)). Where a driver has no previous DOT-regulated employment in those 3 years, the carrier documents that no investigation was possible (391.23(c)(4)). The file rules are in Driver Qualification File.

What the Previous-Employer Investigation Covers

  • Identification and employment verification, and the accident data elements in 390.15(b)(1) for accidents, as defined in 390.5, in the 3 years before the employment application (391.23(d)).
  • Alcohol and controlled substances information from previous DOT-regulated employers where the driver did safety-sensitive work: violations of subpart B of part 382 or part 40, failure to complete a substance abuse professional's rehabilitation program, and certain testing violations after a completed referral (391.23(e)). Since January 6, 2023, employers subject to 382.701(a) must use the Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse to meet this requirement for FMCSA-regulated employers (391.23(e)(4)).
  • Consent: the carrier must give the previous employer the driver's consent that meets 40.321(b), and if the driver refuses, the carrier must not let the driver operate a commercial motor vehicle for it. The same applies to a refusal of consent for a Clearinghouse query (391.23(f)).
  • Method: interviews, calls, letters or any other method the carrier considers appropriate, with a written record of each employer contacted, or of the attempts made, including name, address, date and what was received (391.23(c)(2)). Failures of previous employers to respond should be reported to FMCSA under 386.12 and the report kept in the file (391.23(c)(3)).

What the PSP Is

Section 31150, added in 2005, directs the Secretary of Transportation to give persons conducting pre-employment screening services for the motor carrier industry electronic access to three kinds of reports in the Motor Carrier Management Information System: commercial motor vehicle accident reports, inspection reports that contain no driver-related safety violations, and serious driver-related safety violation inspection reports. A "serious driver-related violation" is one that the Secretary determines will result in the operator being prohibited from continuing to operate a commercial motor vehicle until it is corrected (31150(a), (d)).

Before granting access, the Secretary must ensure that:

  • released information complies with the Fair Credit Reporting Act and other federal law;
  • no screening is done without the operator-applicant's written consent;
  • the information is not released to anyone other than the requesting carrier or the applicant, unless the law expressly authorizes it; and
  • there is a procedure for the applicant to correct inaccurate information in the system in a timely manner (31150(b)).

The process is designed to help carriers assess an individual operator's crash and serious safety violation inspection history. Its use is not mandatory and may be used only during the pre-employment assessment of an operator-applicant (31150(c)).

Not verified, and not in this guide: how many years of crash and inspection history a PSP report covers, its fee, and how a driver or carrier orders one. FMCSA's PSP pages could not be read in a form that allows checking, so those details are left to the official site.

The Driver's Rights

A prospective employer must tell a driver with DOT-regulated employment in the preceding 3 years, on the application form or in another written document before any hiring decision, that the driver may review the information previous employers provide, have errors corrected and re-sent, and have a rebuttal attached if the employer and driver cannot agree (391.23(i)(1)).

  • Review: the driver makes a written request, at any time up to 30 days after being employed or being told of a denial. The prospective employer must supply the information within five business days of the request, or of receiving it from the previous employer if it has not arrived yet. A driver who has not arranged to collect the records within 30 days after they are made available is treated as having waived the request (391.23(i)(2)).
  • Correction: the driver asks the previous employer, which must either correct and forward the information or tell the driver within 15 days that it will not (391.23(j)(1), (2)).
  • Rebuttal: the driver sends it to the previous employer, which within five business days must forward a copy to the prospective employer and append it to the record for the three-year retention period (391.23(j)(3), (4)). A rebuttal can be submitted with or without a correction request (391.23(j)(5)).

A previous employer must respond to a request within 30 days, even if only to confirm there is nothing to report, and must keep a record of each request and response for one year (391.23(g)). The prospective employer may use the information only in deciding whether to hire and must protect it from disclosure (391.23(k)).

When a Background-Check Company Is Involved

If a carrier uses a consumer reporting agency, 15 U.S.C. 1681b(b) applies in addition to the safety regulations:

  • Before the report: a clear and conspicuous written disclosure, in a document that consists solely of the disclosure, that a report may be obtained for employment purposes, and the consumer's written authorization (1681b(b)(2)(A)).
  • Before an adverse action based in whole or in part on the report: a copy of the report and a written description of the consumer's rights (1681b(b)(3)(A)).
  • Applications by mail, telephone or computer for positions covered by the Secretary of Transportation's hours-of-service authority have a separate route with notice and consent by oral, written or electronic means, and a post-action notice within 3 business days (1681b(b)(2)(B), (b)(3)(B)).

The statute does not set a number of days for the period between the pre-adverse notice and the final decision, so none is given here.

Checklist

  • ☐ Written disclosure and authorization on file before any consumer report is ordered
  • ☐ Driver notified of review, correction and rebuttal rights on the application or another written document
  • ☐ Motor vehicle record from each licensing authority, requested and filed within 30 days of the start date
  • ☐ Previous-employer replies, or documented attempts, in the investigation history file within 30 days
  • ☐ Full Clearinghouse query before safety-sensitive work
  • ☐ PSP report, if used, ordered only with the applicant's written consent

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the PSP report required?

No. Section 31150(c) says use of the process is not mandatory.

What if a previous employer does not answer?

The carrier documents its good-faith efforts and keeps them in the investigation history file, and should report the failure to FMCSA (391.23(c)(2), (3)).

Can a PSP report be used after hiring?

The statute says the process may be used only during the pre-employment assessment of an operator-applicant (31150(c)).
