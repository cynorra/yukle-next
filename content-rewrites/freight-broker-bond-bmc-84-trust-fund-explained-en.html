• A freight broker must have a $75,000 surety bond (filed on Form BMC-84) or trust fund (filed on Form BMC-85) in effect before FMCSA registers it, and the registration lasts only as long as the bond or trust fund does (49 CFR 387.307(a); 49 U.S.C. 13906(b)(3)).
  • The security is there to pay claims by shippers or motor carriers when the broker fails to carry out its contracts or to pay freight charges, and a surety that receives a claim notice must respond within 30 days, in writing with grounds if it denies the claim (387.307(a); 13906(b)(2)).
  • If payments push the bond or trust fund below $75,000, the surety or financial institution must notify FMCSA within 2 business days, and FMCSA suspends the broker's authority within 7 business days of its notice unless the shortfall is cured or shown to be an error (387.307(e)).

A freight broker does not own trucks, so a shipper or carrier that deals with one is relying on a promise. The federal financial-security rule is what stands behind that promise. This guide covers what 49 CFR 387.307 and 49 U.S.C. 13906(b) require, what triggers a suspension, and the broker conduct rules in 49 CFR part 371 that matter when checking a counterparty. This is general information, not legal advice.

What the Rule Requires

Section 387.307, which states that it is effective January 16, 2026, requires a broker to have a surety bond or trust fund of $75,000 in effect. FMCSA will not register a broker until security for the full limit is in effect, and the registration remains in effect only as long as the bond or trust fund does. The statute sets the same $75,000 figure regardless of the number of branch offices or sales agents (13906(b)(3)).

OptionForm filed with FMCSAKey detail from the rule
Surety bondBMC-84A surety company provides security for the $75,000 limit.
Trust fundBMC-85Assets totalling $75,000 that can be liquidated to cash within 7 calendar days: cash, irrevocable letters of credit issued by a federally insured depository institution, and Treasury bonds (387.307(b)).

By statute, a trust fund or other financial security is acceptable only if it consists of assets readily available to pay claims without resort to personal guarantees or collection of pledged accounts receivable (13906(b)(1)(C)). The amount may not be reduced by deducting attorney's fees or administrative costs (13906(b)(8)).

Cancellation and Replacement

Either instrument must specify that coverage remains in effect continuously until terminated as the rule allows. It can be cancelled only on 30 days' written notice to FMCSA, using Form BMC-36 for a surety bond and Form BMC-85 for a trust fund, and the notice period starts when FMCSA's Washington, DC office actually receives it (387.307(d)(2)(i)). FMCSA posts the notice on a public website (13906(b)(4)). A bond or trust fund may be replaced by another, which ends the old surety's or trustee's liability from the replacement date, but not for damages from contracts the broker made before that date (387.307(d)(2)(ii)).

The amount of the bond is not what a broker pays for it. The yearly premium is set by the surety, and the federal rules reviewed for this guide do not state it.

What the Security Pays

The statute says the security is available to pay a claim against a broker arising from its failure to pay freight charges under its contracts, agreements or arrangements for transportation, if one of three conditions is met (13906(b)(2)(A)):

  • subject to the surety provider's review, the broker consents to the payment;
  • the broker does not respond to adequate notice to address the validity of the claim, and the surety provider determines the claim is valid; or
  • the claim is not resolved within a reasonable period after a reasonable attempt by the claimant to resolve it under the first two routes, and it has been reduced to a judgment against the broker.

A surety provider that receives notice of such a claim must respond on or before the 30th day after receiving it and, if it denies the claim, set out the grounds in writing (13906(b)(2)(B)). In an action against a surety provider on such a claim, the prevailing party is entitled to reasonable costs and attorney's fees (13906(b)(2)(C)).

When Claims Draw Down the Security

Under 387.307(e), a surety company or financial institution must notify FMCSA in writing, electronically, when it:

  • makes a payment, with the broker's consent, on a shipper or carrier claim that causes the bond or trust fund to fall below $75,000;
  • makes such a payment after the broker did not respond within 7 business days on the claim's validity and the provider found the claim valid;
  • makes a payment on a judgment against the broker that causes the shortfall; or
  • determines that the broker is experiencing financial failure or insolvency and will be required to pay claims that cause the shortfall.

To avoid or lift a suspension the broker must give FMCSA written evidence of one of three things: the notification was sent in error, the bond or trust fund has been restored to $75,000, or the pending claims were satisfied without using bond or trust assets. The statute separately says FMCSA must immediately suspend a broker's registration if its available financial security falls below the required amount (13906(b)(5)). The notification rule does not apply when a broker has filed a proceeding under Title 11 of the United States Code (387.307(e)(2)).

Invented example. A broker holds a $75,000 trust fund. A claim of $20,000 is paid with its consent, leaving $55,000. The payment caused the fund to fall below $75,000, so the financial institution must tell FMCSA within 2 business days, and the broker must restore the fund, show the notice was an error, or satisfy the other claims without trust assets before the 7-business-day period runs out.

Financial Failure or Insolvency

The rule treats a payment or default under 387.307(e)(1) that is not cured as a financial failure or insolvency, and a Title 11 filing alone does not count (387.307(f)(1), (2)). The surety or financial institution then starts cancellation, FMCSA posts a notice in the FMCSA Register, and the provider must accept claims for 60 calendar days after that notice (387.307(f)(3), (4)). By statute, it then pays, not later than 30 days after the 60-day period, all uncontested claims received, or a pro rata share if total claims exceed the security available (13906(b)(6)). For a carrier, this means a broker's registration can lapse because the security was used up, not only because it was cancelled.

Broker Conduct Rules in Part 371

  • Who counts as a broker (371.2): a person who, for compensation, arranges or offers to arrange transportation of property by an authorized motor carrier. A motor carrier, or an employee or bona fide agent of a carrier, is not a broker when arranging shipments the carrier is authorized to transport and has accepted and legally bound itself to transport.
  • Records (371.3): a record of each transaction showing the consignor, the originating carrier with its registration number, the bill of lading or freight bill number, the compensation received and the payer, any non-brokerage service and its compensation, and the freight charges collected and the date paid to the carrier. Records are kept for three years, and each party to a brokered transaction may review its record.
  • Misrepresentation (371.7): no brokerage service or advertising in any name other than the one on the registration, no representing operations as those of a carrier, and advertising must show the broker status.
  • Rebating (371.9): no charging or receiving compensation from a carrier where the broker owns or has a material beneficial interest in the shipment or controls it through common ownership with the shipper, and no giving anything of value to a shipper, consignor or consignee, except inexpensive promotional advertising items.

Checklist Before Accepting a First Load

  • ☐ Confirm the broker's registration and bond or trust fund status in FMCSA's public records
  • ☐ Check that the name on the rate confirmation matches the registered name (371.7)
  • ☐ Ask for the record of any brokered load you hauled (371.3(c))
  • ☐ Keep your own paperwork for at least the three years the broker must keep its records
  • ☐ If a payment is late, send a written claim notice to the broker and the surety early, because the surety's 30-day response clock starts when it receives notice

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the bond protect the broker or the carrier?

It protects shippers and motor carriers with claims for unpaid freight charges (387.307(a); 13906(b)(2)). It is not insurance for the broker.

Can a broker cancel its bond whenever it wants?

No. Cancellation requires 30 days' written notice to FMCSA (387.307(d)(2)(i)).

Is a bonded broker the same as a bona fide dispatcher?

No. A dispatcher's status depends on the definition in 371.2; see Bona Fide Dispatcher vs. Unauthorized Broker.

Scope: the security rule and four broker conduct sections. How a surety handles a particular claim depends on the facts and the contracts. Freight forwarders, household goods brokers and carrier-broker collection disputes are not covered. Related: What Is a Freight Broker?
