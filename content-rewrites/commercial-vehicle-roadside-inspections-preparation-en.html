• A driver subject to the hours rules must be able to show the record of duty status for the previous 7 consecutive days, and since 23 June 2025 a CDL or CLP holder with a current medical certificate no longer has to carry the paper certificate (49 CFR 395.8(k)(2), 391.41(a)(2)).
  • The driver vehicle inspection report is a written end-of-day report listing any defect found, the carrier must repair or certify it before the vehicle runs again, and the report and certification are kept for three months (49 CFR 396.11).
  • A vehicle declared out of service may not be operated until the listed repairs are done, and the carrier must certify the corrections within 15 days of the inspection and keep a copy for 12 months (49 CFR 396.9).

A roadside inspection checks a driver and a vehicle against fixed rules, so most of what an inspector looks for can be prepared in advance. The rules that decide what a driver must show, what a vehicle's paperwork must hold and what happens after a violation are short. This guide sets them out with the date-stamped changes, a pre-trip checklist built from the rule text, and a dated out-of-service example. It does not reproduce the inspection levels or the out-of-service criteria published by the Commercial Vehicle Safety Alliance (CVSA), which are copyrighted publications that could not be verified for this guide; 49 CFR 390.5 refers to them by name.

What an Inspection Produces

Under 49 CFR 396.9(a) and (b), FMCSA special agents may enter and inspect a motor carrier's vehicles in operation, and the Driver Vehicle Examination Report records the results. If the vehicle's mechanical condition or loading would likely cause an accident or breakdown, the inspector must declare and mark it out of service with an "Out-of-Service Vehicle" sticker. A driver may also be declared out of service; "out-of-service order" in 390.5 covers a driver, a vehicle or a carrier operation.

What the Driver Must Be Able to Show

LicenseA person may not operate a commercial motor vehicle that requires a CDL without holding one (49 CFR 383.23(a)(2)).
Record of duty statusEach driver subject to the hours rules records duty status per 24-hour period, on an ELD unless a paper exception applies, and must keep a copy of the previous 7 consecutive days available while on duty (395.8(a), (k)(2)). Paper logs are allowed for drivers who need a record on no more than 8 days in any 30, in certain driveaway-towaway operations, and for vehicles manufactured before model year 2000 (395.8(a)(1)(ii)).

A driver qualified through an FMCSA medical variance still carries a copy of the variance documentation (391.41(a)(1)(ii)).
Annual inspection proofDocumentation that each component passed an inspection in the preceding 12 months must be on the vehicle: the inspection report, or a sticker or decal with the inspection date, the inspecting entity's name and address, vehicle identification and a certification (396.17(c)).

Registration and permits for particular loads depend on the State and the cargo and are not covered here.

The Driver Vehicle Inspection Report

Under 396.11, the carrier must require its drivers, and each driver must, prepare a written report at the end of each day's work on each vehicle operated. It covers at least: service brakes including trailer brake connections, the parking brake, the steering mechanism, lighting devices and reflectors, tires, the horn, windshield wipers, rear vision mirrors, coupling devices, wheels and rims, and emergency equipment. It must identify the vehicle and list any defect or deficiency that would affect safe operation or cause a breakdown, and the driver signs it. A driver need not prepare a report if no defect is found.

  • Repair or certify. Before the vehicle is operated again, the carrier or its agent must repair any listed defect likely to affect safe operation and certify on the report that it was repaired or that repair was unnecessary.
  • Before driving (396.13). The driver must be satisfied the vehicle is in safe operating condition, review the last report, and sign it to acknowledge the review and the certification of repairs.
  • Keeping it. The report, the repair certification and the driver's review certification are kept for three months from the date of the report.
  • Exceptions. The section does not apply to a private motor carrier of passengers (nonbusiness), a driveaway-towaway operation, or a carrier operating only one commercial motor vehicle.
  • Electronic. Reports may be created and kept electronically under 390.32.

Pre-Trip Checklist

  • ☐ Last report reviewed, its repair certification seen, and signed (396.13)
  • ☐ Brakes, parking brake, steering, lights and reflectors, tires, wheels and rims, horn, wipers, mirrors, coupling devices and emergency equipment looked at (the 396.11 list)
  • ☐ Load secured and checked as required (Cargo Securement; first check within 50 miles, 392.9)
  • ☐ Weight within limits (Weight Limits)
  • ☐ Hours: duty status current and the plan fits the limits (Hours of Service)
  • ☐ Annual inspection proof on the vehicle (396.17)

If a Vehicle Is Put Out of Service

  • No one may operate it, and no carrier may require or permit it, until the repairs required by the notice are satisfactorily completed. "Operate" includes towing, except that a vehicle may be towed away using a crane or hoist (396.9(c)(2)).
  • No one may remove the sticker before the required repairs are complete (396.9(c)(3)).
  • The driver delivers a copy of the report to the carrier at the next terminal or facility, or transmits it immediately if the driver will not reach one within 24 hours (396.9(d)(1)).
  • Within 15 days of the inspection the carrier certifies that all violations were corrected, by completing the signature portion of the form. If the issuing State agency asks for the form back, the carrier returns it; in all cases it keeps a copy for 12 months (396.9(d)(3)). The return-on-request wording came from an amendment published 22 June 2026.

A dated example (invented): a vehicle is inspected and marked out of service on 3 November 2026. The carrier's certification is due by 18 November 2026 (15 days), and it keeps a copy until 3 November 2027 (12 months).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a paper medical certificate?

Not if you are required to hold a CDL or CLP and have a current certificate on file, since 23 June 2025. A non-CDL driver still carries the certificate or a copy; a variance holder carries the variance documentation.

Is a DVIR required every day?

A written report is required at the end of each day's work, but a driver need not prepare one if no defect is found (396.11(a)(2)(i)).

Who certifies the repairs after an out-of-service order?

The motor carrier, within 15 days, on the signature portion of the inspection form (396.9(d)(3)).

Scope: the federal rules for property carriers. CVSA levels and criteria, State requirements, and the effect of violations on a carrier's safety record are outside this guide, and none of it is legal advice.
