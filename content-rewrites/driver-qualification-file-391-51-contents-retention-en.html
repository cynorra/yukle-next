• A carrier must keep a driver qualification file for each driver it employs, and the file stays until the driver has left plus three years, with the annual motor vehicle record, review note and medical records removable three years after their own dates (49 CFR 391.51).
  • Within 30 days of the start date the carrier must have the driver's motor vehicle record in the file and the previous-employer safety history, or its good-faith attempts, in a separate driver investigation history file with controlled access (391.23, 391.53).
  • The Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse is a separate check: a full pre-employment query before safety-sensitive work, at least one query a year, and a full query within 24 hours if a limited query shows information exists (382.701).

A driver qualification file is the carrier's record that a commercial driver is allowed to drive for it. An FMCSA audit often starts there, and a missing item is one of the easiest findings to avoid. The rule is 49 CFR 391.51. Below are what goes in the file, what goes in a second file beside it, the annual check, the Clearinghouse queries, how long to keep each record, and a calculator for the dates.

Who Keeps One

Under 391.51(a), each motor carrier must maintain a driver qualification file for each driver it employs, and the file may be combined with the personnel file. The rule is addressed to the carrier. The definition of "employee" in 390.5 includes an independent contractor while operating a commercial motor vehicle, so a carrier that uses leased owner-operators should confirm with FMCSA how part 391 applies to them; this guide does not decide it.

Two medical provisions carry dates. For CDL holders, 391.51(b)(6)(ii) allowed a carrier to use a copy of the driver's current medical examiner's certificate, submitted to the State, as proof for up to 15 days after issue, through 22 June 2025. And 391.51(b)(8)(ii) required a National Registry verification note for drivers who needed a CDL, again through 22 June 2025. Both date limits have passed in the text read for this guide. Copies that are legible and accurately reflect the record may be kept in place of originals (390.31); the text read does not address electronic storage, so confirm that separately.

The Investigation History File

Records of the investigation into a new driver's safety performance history over the previous three years are kept in a second file, the driver investigation history file (391.53), in a secure location with controlled access. The carrier must place the replies, or documentation of good-faith efforts, in it within 30 days of the start of employment (391.23(c)). The file holds the driver's written authorization to seek alcohol and controlled substances history and the previous employers' responses, each with the employer's name and address, the date of contact and the information received. Access is limited to those involved in the hiring decision or who control access, and the carrier's insurer may see it except the alcohol and controlled substances data. The histories of a driver who is hired are kept for as long as the driver is employed plus three years (391.53(c)).

The Annual Check

At least once every 12 months, under 391.25, the carrier makes an inquiry for each driver's motor vehicle record covering at least the preceding 12 months, from each licensing authority where the driver held a commercial motor vehicle license or permit, and reviews it to decide whether the driver still meets the minimum requirements for safe driving or is disqualified under 391.15. The review must consider evidence of violations of the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations or Hazardous Materials Regulations and the driver's accident record, and give great weight to violations such as speeding, reckless driving and driving under the influence. The record and a note with the reviewer's name and the date go in the file.

Clearinghouse Queries

  • Before hire: a driver subject to testing may not be used for safety-sensitive work without a pre-employment query. It must be a full query, which requires the driver's specific consent (382.701(a)).
  • Each year: at least one query for every driver subject to testing. A limited query, which only says whether information exists, can replace the full query with the driver's consent, and that consent can last more than a year (382.701(b)).
  • The 24-hour step: if a limited query shows information exists, a full query is due within 24 hours, and until it is done and shows no prohibition the driver may not continue safety-sensitive work.
  • After a query: if information is entered during the 30 days that follow a query, FMCSA notifies the employer (382.701(c)).
  • Records: keep a record of each query and the response for 3 years; as of 6 January 2023, an employer with a valid Clearinghouse registration fulfils this (382.701(e)).

How Long to Keep Records

The file stays for as long as the driver is employed and for three years afterward (391.51(c)). Five items may be removed three years after their dates of execution: the annual motor vehicle record, the annual review note, the medical examiner's certificate or CDLIS record, any medical variance or exemption, and the National Registry verification note (391.51(d)).

Common mistakes: counting three years from the hire date instead of the end of employment, removing the application and the hire-date motor vehicle record early, and mixing the previous-employer responses into the qualification file instead of the investigation history file.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the investigation history part of the qualification file?

It is a separate file under 391.53, with controlled access, though the 30-day timing runs from the same start date.

Can the carrier use a limited Clearinghouse query before hiring?

No. The pre-employment query must be a full query with the driver's specific consent. A limited query can satisfy only the annual requirement.

Do records have to be originals?

Legible copies that accurately reflect the information may be kept in place of originals (390.31).
