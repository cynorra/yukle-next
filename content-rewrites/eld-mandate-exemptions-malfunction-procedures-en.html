• A carrier whose drivers must record duty status has to install and require an electronic logging device (ELD) that meets 49 CFR 395 subpart B and appears on FMCSA's registered ELD list, except where a driver falls in one of the four paper-log cases in 395.8(a)(1)(ii)(A) or outside the rule altogether (395.8(a)(1)(i); 395.22(a)).
  • When an ELD malfunctions, the driver must note it and give the carrier written notice within 24 hours, reconstruct the current 24 hours and the previous 7 days on paper graph-grid logs, and keep logging on paper until the ELD is brought back into compliance (395.34(a)).
  • The carrier must correct the malfunction within 8 days of discovery or the driver's notice, whichever is first, and a carrier that needs longer must notify the FMCSA Division Administrator within 5 days after the driver's notice (395.34(d)).

The electronic logging device rule replaced most paper logbooks with a recording device that is tied to the vehicle's engine. Two parts of 49 CFR part 395 do the work: 395.8, which says who must record duty status and how, and subpart B, which sets the standards for the device itself. This guide covers who must use an ELD, the narrow cases where paper is still allowed, what the driver and the carrier do when a device fails, how records are handed over at the roadside, and how long they are kept. This is general information, not legal advice.

Who Must Use an ELD

A motor carrier subject to part 395 must require each driver to record duty status for each 24-hour period, other than a private motor carrier of passengers (nonbusiness). The carrier must install and require its drivers to use an ELD in accordance with subpart B (395.8(a)(1)(i)). An ELD is a device or technology that automatically records a driver's driving time and facilitates accurate recording of hours of service (395.2). Drivers record one of four statuses: off duty, sleeper berth, driving, or on-duty not driving (395.24(b)).

When Paper Is Still Allowed

Under 395.8(a)(1)(ii)(A), a carrier may require a driver to record duty status manually, rather than use an ELD, if the driver is operating a commercial motor vehicle:

CaseText of the rule
Eight days in thirtyIn a manner requiring completion of a record of duty status on not more than 8 days within any 30-day period
Driveaway-towaway, vehicle is the shipmentIn a driveaway-towaway operation in which the vehicle being driven is part of the shipment being delivered
Driveaway-towaway, motor home or recreation vehicle trailerIn a driveaway-towaway operation in which the vehicle being transported is a motor home or a recreation vehicle trailer
Older vehicleThat was manufactured before model year 2000, as reflected in the vehicle identification number as shown on the vehicle's registration

The model-year test looks at the vehicle, not the engine. The paper record is made in duplicate for each 24-hour period, on the grid in 395.8(g) (395.8(a)(1)(ii)(B)). The short-haul rule in 395.1(e)(1) works differently: it exempts a qualifying driver from 395.8 and 395.11 altogether, rather than allowing a paper log. A driver with a 150 air-mile radius from the normal work reporting location must be released from work within 14 consecutive hours, and a property-carrying driver needs at least 10 consecutive hours off duty between 14-hour on-duty periods, among other conditions. Other exceptions in 395.1, such as agricultural operations, are not covered here.

What the Carrier Must Do

  • Use only a registered ELD: one listed on FMCSA's registered ELDs list (395.22(a)).
  • Calibrate and maintain the device to the provider's specifications, and mount a portable ELD in a fixed position visible to the seated driver (395.22(f), (g)).
  • Keep an information packet in the vehicle: an instruction sheet on data transfer, an instruction sheet on malfunction reporting and recordkeeping, and enough blank graph-grid logs for at least 8 days (395.22(h)).
  • Back up ELD records for 6 months on a separate device and produce them electronically on request (395.22(i), (j)).
  • No tampering: no driver or carrier may make a false duty-status report, or disable, jam or tamper with an ELD so it does not accurately record, or permit or require another person to do so (395.8(e)).

Malfunction and Data Diagnostic Events

The rules separate two things. A malfunction affects the device's compliance and triggers the steps below. A data diagnostic event is an inconsistency flagged by the ELD, and the driver follows the carrier's and the ELD provider's recommendations to resolve it (395.34(c)). The device sets a malfunction itself when it detects one of these conditions (appendix A to subpart B, section 4.6.1):

MalfunctionWhen the ELD must set it
Power complianceAggregated in-motion driving time understatement of 30 minutes or more over a 24-hour period across all driver profiles
Engine synchronizationConnectivity to a required engine data source lost for more than 30 minutes in a 24-hour period
TimingThe ELD can no longer meet its time-accuracy requirement against an external UTC source
PositioningMore than a cumulative 60 minutes in 24 hours without a valid position measurement within 5 miles of the vehicle's movement
Data recordingThe ELD can no longer record or retain required events or retrieve logs not otherwise stored remotely by the carrier
Data transferThe device stays in unconfirmed data transfer mode after three consecutive daily checks that follow a data transfer diagnostic event

The ELD must show a visual malfunction indicator that the seated driver can see (appendix A, 4.6.2.1).

The Driver's Steps (395.34(a), (b))

  1. Note the malfunction and give the carrier written notice within 24 hours.
  2. Reconstruct the record of duty status for the current 24-hour period and the previous 7 consecutive days on graph-grid paper logs that meet 395.8, unless the driver already has the records or they can be retrieved from the ELD.
  3. Keep preparing paper logs until the ELD is serviced and back in compliance.
  4. If inspected during the malfunction, hand the inspector the paper records.

The Carrier's Steps (395.34(d))

A carrier that learns of a malfunction must take action to correct it within 8 days of discovery or of the driver's notification, whichever comes first. To get more time, it must notify the FMCSA Division Administrator for the State of its principal place of business within 5 days after the driver's notification. The signed request must name the carrier representative, identify each ELD and malfunction, and describe the good-faith repair steps and why more than 8 days is needed. FMCSA may allow an additional period and will give written notice of its decision, which is a final agency action. A carrier whose request meets those requirements is deemed in compliance with 395.8(a)(1)(i) and (a)(2) until FMCSA decides, as long as the carrier and driver keep to the other steps.

Invented exampleDate
Driver notes a malfunction and notifies the carrier in writing3 March
Last day to give notice of an extension request to the Division Administrator (5 days after notification)8 March

The 8 days run from discovery or from the driver's notification, whichever comes first, so a carrier that finds the fault before the driver reports it starts that clock sooner. The 5-day notice for an extension runs from the driver's notification.

Unidentified Driving

When no driver is logged in, the ELD records driving under an "Unidentified Driver" account (395.32(a)). A driver who logs in must review any unassigned driving time and either assume it or mark it as not attributable. The carrier must review unidentified driving records, annotate why the time is unassigned or assign it to the right driver, keep the records for 6 months, and make them available during a safety inspection or audit (395.32).

Roadside Data Transfer

On request by an authorized safety official, a driver must produce and transfer hours-of-service records from the ELD following the instruction sheet the carrier supplied (395.24(d)). Under appendix A, an ELD must support at least one of two options, and the official specifies which mechanism within the options the device certifies:

  • Telematics: both wireless web services and email.
  • Local: both USB 2.0 and Bluetooth.

The ELD must also be able to show the current 24-hour period and the previous 7 consecutive days on a display or printout (appendix A, 4.9.1).

Supporting Documents and Retention

An ELD does not replace supporting documents. A carrier must keep, for each driver and each 24-hour period, documents in five categories: bills of lading, itineraries and similar documents showing the origin and destination of each trip; dispatch and trip records; expense receipts related to on-duty not driving time; electronic mobile communication records; and payroll or settlement records (395.11(c)). A carrier need not keep more than eight per driver per 24-hour period (395.11(d)), and drivers submit supporting documents within 13 days (395.11(b)). Records of duty status and supporting documents are kept for not less than 6 months from receipt, and the driver keeps a copy of the previous 7 consecutive days of records on duty (395.8(k)). A driver may access their own ELD records, and a carrier may not require them to go through the carrier to get records the ELD makes available (395.36).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the short-haul exemption count as an ELD exemption?

No. It exempts the driver from 395.8 and 395.11 if its conditions are met (395.1(e)(1)). The four paper-log cases are in 395.8(a)(1)(ii)(A).

Is the pre-2000 rule based on the engine year?

No. The rule refers to the vehicle's model year, as shown by the VIN on its registration (395.8(a)(1)(ii)(A)(4)).
