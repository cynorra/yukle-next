• The federal public-liability floor in 49 CFR 387.9 depends on what is hauled and how: $750,000 for for-hire carriers of non-hazardous property in interstate or foreign commerce with a GVWR of 10,001 lb or more, $1,000,000 for oil and most hazardous materials, and $5,000,000 for the highest-risk categories.
  • Many carriers sit outside the table entirely, including private carriers of non-hazardous goods, for-hire carriers that stay within one State, and vehicles under 10,001 lb GVWR, though State law or a customer's contract can still require insurance.
  • Proof of coverage is public information, must be in the carrier's exact registered name, and stays in force until cancelled on 35 days' written notice.

Federal law sets a floor for the liability insurance a motor carrier must hold, and the floor is set by the cargo, the vehicle's weight rating and the kind of operation, not by the size of the company. This guide reads the table and the rules around it, lets you pick the row that applies, and lists what proof a carrier has to keep and how a customer can check it.

What the Rule Covers

Under 387.7(a), no motor carrier may operate a motor vehicle until it has in effect the minimum financial responsibility set in 387.9. The thing insured is public liability, defined in 387.5 as liability for bodily injury or property damage, including liability for environmental restoration. It protects people and property harmed by the vehicle. It is not cargo insurance and not coverage for the carrier's own truck, and 387.9 sets no minimum for either.

The table changed in July 2026, but the amounts did not. 387.9 was amended on 21 July 2026 (91 FR 45660). Comparing the eCFR text from before and after that date shows one edit: the Row 2 description lost the phrase "with capacities in bulk" after "hopper-type vehicles" and one comma changed. The dollar figures and the categories are the same.

Two definitions in 387.5 decide which row fits. "For-hire carriage" means transporting, for compensation, the goods or property of another. "In bulk" generally means transporting property in containment systems with capacities above 3,500 water gallons, with separate definitions for the explosive and Hazard Zone A categories, which count in any quantity.

Who Is Outside the Table

387.3 says the subpart applies to for-hire carriers of property in interstate or foreign commerce, and to carriers of hazardous materials, substances or wastes in interstate, foreign or intrastate commerce. It excepts vehicles with a GVWR under 10,001 lb, except when they carry the explosive, Hazard Zone A or Class 7 categories in interstate or foreign commerce. It also excepts non-bulk oil and hazardous materials moved intrastate, except highway route controlled quantities of Class 7. So a private carrier hauling its own non-hazardous goods, or a for-hire carrier that never leaves its State with non-hazardous goods, has no figure in 387.9. That does not mean no insurance is required: State law may set a minimum, and customers often do.

Pick the Row

Rule used: 387.3 decides whether the vehicle is covered; 387.9 gives the amount. Common mistakes: treating the $750,000 figure as a floor for every carrier, ignoring the interstate condition, and assuming a hazardous load is always $1,000,000 when bulk categories carry $5,000,000.

A federal floor is not a contract limit. If a broker's carrier agreement asks for $1,000,000 and the carrier's Row 1 minimum is $750,000, the carrier needs $1,000,000 to take that load. The extra $250,000 is a contract term, not a federal requirement.

Proof, Cancellation and Checking

  • Kinds of proof. Under 387.7(d), the carrier keeps at its principal place of business an MCS-90 endorsement issued by an insurer, an MCS-82 surety bond, or an FMCSA decision authorizing self-insurance under 387.309, provided it keeps a satisfactory safety rating.
  • Public information. The proof is to be produced on reasonable request by a member of the public (387.7(e)(1)).
  • Exact name. The endorsement and surety bond must be issued in the exact name of the motor carrier (387.15).
  • Continuity. Policies, bonds and endorsements stay in effect until terminated. Either side may cancel on 35 days' written notice, counted from the date it is sent. A finite-term policy may cover a lapse (387.7(b)).
  • The insurer. A policy counts only if the insurer is legally authorized to issue it in each State where the carrier operates, or meets one of the alternatives in 387.11, such as designating an agent for service of process.
  • Foreign carriers. A vehicle of a carrier domiciled in a contiguous foreign country must carry a legible English copy of its MCS-90 or MCS-82 and can be denied entry without it (387.7(f), (g)). A Mexico-domiciled carrier working only in U.S. border municipalities or commercial zones with a part 368 registration may use coverage bought for periods of 24 hours or longer (387.7(b)(3)).

To check a carrier, use FMCSA's Licensing and Insurance Carrier Search and ask for the certificate itself; the step-by-step is in How to Check a Trucking Company Before You Hire It. A broker's separate $75,000 financial security is covered in Freight Broker Bond and Trust Fund Rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a box truck need $750,000?

If it is for-hire, in interstate or foreign commerce, rated 10,001 lb or more and hauls non-hazardous property, yes: that is Row 1. A box truck outside those conditions has no figure in the table. See Box Trucks, Cargo Vans and Pickups: Federal Rules by Weight.

Does this cover my freight if it is damaged?

No. 387.9 is public liability. Cargo claims are governed by the carrier's liability and any declared value; see Freight Claims.

Can a carrier operate with a lapsed policy?

387.7(a) says no carrier may operate until it has the minimum in effect. A lapse is a reason to stop before tendering freight.

Are State minimums higher?

They can differ for vehicles that stay in one State. State figures are outside this guide and were not checked.

Scope: this is a reading of the federal rule, not insurance or legal advice. A licensed agent can say what applies to a particular operation.
